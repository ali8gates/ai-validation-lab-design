# Roadmap

Phased by evidence, not by calendar. Each phase has an entry condition, a definition of done, and a reason it might not clear.

## Phase 0. Concept and framing

**Done when:** problem statements validated across buyer and developer sides, product structure defined, governance model drafted, initial data access path identified.

**Status:** complete. The output is the material in this repository.

## Phase 1. First studies

**Entry:** one data access path working end to end, a locked protocol template, a review panel seated.

**Scope:** a small number of cardiovascular models. Structured risk models and signal based tools first, since the data is available and the clinical endpoints are settled.

**Done when:**
- Three to five completed validations with published reports.
- At least one unfavorable or heavily conditioned result published, which is the real test of whether the program is independent.
- One impact analysis completed with a health system.
- Turnaround time per study measured and known.

**Risks:** data access takes longer than the analysis, every time. Protocol negotiation with the first developers sets precedent, so early concessions are permanent. If the first cohort of studies is all favorable, the market will read it correctly.

## Phase 2. Expansion

**Entry:** phase one turnaround is predictable and the review panel is not the bottleneck.

**Scope:**
- Additional modalities, including imaging.
- Registry linked cohorts brought into evaluation flows.
- Monitoring offered as a standing subscription rather than a custom engagement.
- Evidence packaging offered as a standard add on.

**Done when:**
- Monitoring running continuously on validated models with at least one triggered re-review.
- Registry linked studies producing results the de-identified extract path could not.
- Second and third studies for the same developer costing meaningfully less than the first.

**Risks:** monitoring is a recurring obligation with recurring cost, and it will be priced too low at first. Imaging studies are an order of magnitude heavier than record based ones and should not be scoped against phase one experience.

## Phase 3. Standard setting

**Entry:** a published body of work large enough that the methodology itself is the reference point.

**Scope:**
- Evaluation methodology published as a standard others can apply.
- Federated and in place evaluation with individual systems, including small and rural sites.
- Coordination with other clinical societies on shared methodology beyond cardiovascular.
- Results feeding purchasing decisions, guideline work, and payer conversations.

**Done when:** hospitals ask developers for a validation result as a matter of course, and developers arrive expecting to need one.

**Risks:** this phase depends entirely on the credibility built in phases one and two. One compromised result undoes it. Regulatory scope may also shift underneath the program, so the methodology has to stay compatible with whatever oversight arrives rather than competing with it.

## Sequencing rules

- Do not add a modality before turnaround on the current one is predictable.
- Do not add an offering before the one underneath it is repeatable.
- Do not scale the queue faster than the review panel can genuinely read the analysis.
- Keep a fixed share of capacity for under-resourced hospitals in every phase. If that is treated as spare capacity, it will never be available.

## What would tell us this is not working

- Turnaround per study is not improving after the first five.
- Developers publish the mark and not the limitations, and nothing happens to them.
- Health systems read the reports and still cannot make a decision from them.
- Every published result is favorable.
